Good afternoon.

Last night I told you that the Chancellor would address you today at midday with the full detail of the emergency measures we have been preparing. Before I hand to him, one thing.

Denmark and the Netherlands activated their own emergency payment systems yesterday. France and Canada begin this afternoon. Australia and Japan have announced measures for later this week. What the Chancellor is about to set out is consistent with what our partner nations are deploying. We are not alone in this.

Speaker change The Chancellor of the Exchequer

Thank you, Prime Minister.

I said on Saturday morning that I would set out the detail of our emergency payment infrastructure today. I said it would put money in your hands, on your devices, regardless of what has happened to the banking system. Here is that detail.

The Emergency Payment Module is live. As I speak, every ARC-enabled device in the United Kingdom is being credited with two hundred pounds. If your phone is beside you, check it now. You will see a notification from ARC Emergency. That money is yours.

Let me be precise about what this is and what it is not. It is not a loan. You will not be asked to repay it. It is not an advance on wages or benefits. It is an emergency allocation from this Government to you, directly. There is no application. There is no form. There is no means test. Every device receives the same amount.

I want to answer the questions I know you are asking.

How it works

You use your phone exactly as you have always used it. You hold it to the payment terminal. You hear the beep. The transaction is confirmed. The amount is deducted from your EPM balance, which you can see at any time in ARC Emergency. The terminals are the same terminals you have always used. Every payment terminal in this country — in supermarkets, in pharmacies, in petrol stations, in corner shops, in pubs — has been ARC-enabled since September of last year under the Financial Conduct Authority’s interoperability mandate. The infrastructure is already there. We are using it.

Where to spend it

Everywhere. Any terminal that accepted a contactless payment before this crisis will accept EPM today. From two o’clock this afternoon, major retailers will reopen — Tesco, Sainsbury’s, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose, Aldi, Lidl, the Co-op. Boots and Superdrug will reopen for pharmacy services and essential items. Designated fuel stations are already operating and will continue. Your ARC Local app now contains a live map of open retailers and their operating hours in your area. Hours will vary — most major stores will operate from two o’clock until eight o’clock this evening, and from eight until eight from tomorrow onward.

Family devices

Every ARC-enabled device receives its own allocation. If your household has three phones, your household has received six hundred pounds. Children’s devices receive the same amount. I know this is imperfect — a single pensioner with one phone receives two hundred, while a family of four with four phones receives eight hundred. We chose speed and simplicity over precision. The Community Recovery Hubs exist to close that gap, and I will come to them in a moment.

Prescriptions

Pharmacies reopening this afternoon will dispense NHS prescriptions. If you have a repeat prescription, bring your most recent packaging or a note of the medication name and dosage. Pharmacists are authorised to issue emergency supplies of regular medication for up to thirty days without a prescription, under the Emergency Supply provisions. If you are running low on essential medication, please go to your pharmacy this afternoon. Do not wait.

When it runs out

This is the first allocation, not the last. Further allocations will follow on a regular cycle. I will confirm the schedule and the amount by the end of this week. No one in this country will be left without the means to buy food and essentials. That is my commitment.

Transaction limits

No. You can spend your balance in a single transaction or across as many as you wish. There is no minimum and no cap per purchase. When your balance reaches zero, it will be refreshed at the next allocation.

Military presence at retail sites

Military personnel are present at major retail sites. They are there to help — to manage queues, to assist with logistics, to ensure deliveries reach the shelves. They are not there for security and they are not there for enforcement. They are squaddies helping stack shelves and carry boxes. If you see a uniform in the car park, that person is on your side.

Stock

I will be honest with you. Shelves will be patchy. Supply chains that rely on digital invoicing and automated ordering have been disrupted. Fresh produce will be limited. Bread, milk, eggs, tinned goods, rice, pasta, and frozen food are available in reasonable quantities at most major stores. The situation will improve daily as distribution recovers. Please buy what you need. Please do not buy more than you need.

Prices

Price controls are in effect under the Civil Contingencies Act for the duration of this emergency.

Now — for those without a smartphone. I know there are many of you, particularly among our older citizens, those in rural communities, and those whose phones were damaged, lost, or simply never owned. You have not been forgotten.

From tomorrow morning, Community Recovery Hubs across the country will begin distributing emergency food hampers. These are free. No identification is required. You walk in, you receive a hamper — tinned goods, rice, pasta, milk, tea, cooking oil, toiletries, batteries, candles. The basics. Hot food and drinks are available at most hubs now and will continue. If you have an older relative or a neighbour without a phone, please help them find their nearest hub.

Wages, pensions, and savings

I will address these in full on Wednesday once the NCSC has completed its data integrity assessment. For now, the position is unchanged — the obligations freeze protects you from any consequence of non-payment, and EPM provides the means to buy what you need. The bridge will hold.

Prime Minister.

These measures are not temporary.

Speaker change The Prime Minister

Thank you, Chancellor.

One week ago the country woke up to something unprecedented. Five days ago we believed the worst was over. Two days ago we learned it was not. I will not dress that up.

But I want you to notice what just happened. The Chancellor has put money in your hands. Not next week. Not tomorrow. Now. The system he has described — the terminals, the hubs, the logistics, the payment infrastructure — was deployed in seven days by the men and women of the Treasury, the National Cyber Security Centre, the ARC programme, the armed forces, and thousands of local authority workers and volunteers across this country.

On the security situation. The National Cyber Security Centre continues to assess the full scope and origin of this attack. I have received a briefing this morning and I want to be straightforward with you. We have no intelligence to suggest a third phase is imminent. But we have not yet established the full capability of the actor responsible, and until we have, we must proceed with caution. That is why certain digital services remain restricted. That is why the measures the Chancellor has set out today are built on infrastructure that does not depend on the systems that were attacked.

These measures are not temporary. They are designed to hold for as long as they are needed. And they are designed to function whatever comes next.

This afternoon, go to the shops. Buy what you need for the week. If you have a neighbour who cannot get there, ask them what they need. If someone near you does not have a phone, walk them to the hub tomorrow morning.

This address has been broadcast live on BBC television and radio, and on ARC Emergency and ARC Local.

Stay local. Stay safe. Look after each other.

Be well.
