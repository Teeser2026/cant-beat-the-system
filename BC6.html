The Prime Minister
The Prime Minister

Good evening.

I was due to address you at six o’clock this evening. Shortly before that broadcast, at approximately half past five, financial systems across twenty-three nations experienced a further phase of disruption. The United Kingdom is one of those nations. I took the decision to delay this address so that I could instead chair an emergency COBRA session and speak to my counterparts in the affected nations. I wanted to come to you with information, not speculation.

AssessmentThe restoration of payment and banking services that had been progressing well over the past forty-eight hours has suffered a significant and unexpected setback. Our current assessment is that a second phase of the original attack was embedded within the systems themselves, and was triggered by the process of reconnection. The National Cyber Security Centre is working through the night to establish the full technical picture.

As a precaution, cloud services that were in the process of being restored have been taken back offline. This includes platforms and services that were cleared and returned to operation earlier today. I understand the frustration of that. But until we understand the nature of what we are dealing with, we cannot leave those systems running. The NCSC has made this recommendation and I have accepted it.

I know many of you experienced this directly this evening. In shops. At terminals. On your phones. I know many of you have seen messages on your banking applications using the word “permanently.” I want to address that. Those messages were generated automatically by systems detecting that their connection to verified records has been interrupted. They describe a technical condition at this moment in time. They are not a statement of fact about your money and they are not a prediction.

I cannot confirm that timetable tonight. We are, in many respects, back to where we were at the start of this week.

TimelineI told you on Monday that we were looking at days, not weeks. Last night I told you we were on track. That schools would reopen on Monday. That businesses would follow.

I cannot confirm that timetable tonight. We are, in many respects, back to where we were at the start of this week. The progress we made was real. The systems that were restored were functioning. What happened this evening does not undo the work that was done — but it does change the picture, and I need my security and technical advisers to complete their assessment before I can set out a new timetable. I will not give you a timeline I cannot stand behind.

What has not changedOur essential services are unaffected. Hospitals are open. Emergency services are responding. Power, water and gas supplies are secure. The emergency measures already in place remain in place.

Schools will not reopen on Monday as planned. ARC Education will continue and we will update parents as the position becomes clearer.

Shops and supermarkets will not open tomorrow. All other businesses, hospitality and leisure venues remain closed until further notice. Your Community Recovery Hub will provide food and essential supplies.

For the time being, all non-essential travel should stop. Stay local. Stay close to your families. Check on your neighbours, particularly those living alone. If you need help, your local Community Recovery Hub remains open.

ARC Emergency and ARC Local will continue to provide live updates through the night and into tomorrow. My colleagues and I will address you again at nine o’clock tomorrow morning with a full assessment of where we stand.

This is a setback. It is a serious one. But we have the infrastructure, the people and the resolve to deal with it. We dealt with the first shock. We will deal with this one.

Stay safe. Stay local.
