The Prime Minister

Good evening.

I want to begin by saying that the country has had a calm and patient day. Two days into this disruption, the streets are orderly, hospitals are open, emergency services are responding normally, and the early signs from our restoration work are good. I am grateful for that patience, and I will not over-thank you for it. We have a job to do, and we are getting on with it.

I am joined this evening, as I said I would be, by the Chancellor of the Exchequer and by Paul Chichester, the Director of Operations at the National Cyber Security Centre. The Chancellor will set out the practical measures and the restoration timeline. Paul will explain, in plain terms, what has happened and what is being done. I will close.

Chancellor.

Speaker change The Chancellor of the Exchequer

Thank you, Prime Minister.

I am going to focus on three things this evening. The restoration plan and what to expect tomorrow. The practical arrangements for the days in between. And the support measures that are now in place.

RestorationThe 24-hour assessment window the Prime Minister announced last night has now concluded. The picture from that assessment is broadly positive. The disruption is at the authentication layer — the cryptographic system that verifies who is who when a card is presented at a terminal, or when you log in to your bank. The legacy cryptography that has underpinned that layer for thirty years has been compromised. The newer authentication framework — already deployed across UK payment infrastructure under regulation from last year — has not been. Our work since yesterday morning has been to verify each banking application, each card network, and each payment acquirer's systems, and to bring them back online on the new framework, in sequence, as each is confirmed safe.

That work is moving on schedule. The largest UK card networks expect to begin processing transactions through tomorrow morning, with the major UK banks following through the day as their core systems complete verification. We expect to see meaningful progress through Wednesday and Thursday, and the great majority of the retail payment estate to be operational by the weekend. The Prime Minister will give a detailed restoration update tomorrow evening at six o'clock.

To be clear about what this means in practice tomorrow: some terminals at some retailers will accept some cards from some banks. Coverage will broaden through the day. If your card does not work at a particular terminal in the morning, please do not assume the system is broken — it may simply not yet have been restored. Cash will continue to be the most reliable payment method until coverage is widespread.

Essential retailFrom seven o'clock tomorrow morning, the network of designated retail outlets that opened this morning will expand significantly. More stores in more locations. Longer opening hours. Major supermarket chains, additional pharmacies, post offices, and selected hardware and home essentials stores will join the designated network. The full updated list is being distributed tonight through ARC Emergency, the BBC, and printed notices at every Community Recovery Hub. Cash payments only, until card coverage is restored. A household member may visit once per day.

FuelA designated fuel network begins today, expanding to additional stations from tomorrow. Cash only, with rationing in place, a limit of £30 per vehicle. The list is being distributed alongside the retail list. Please use the designated stations, and please travel only if you need to.

CashFrom ten o'clock tomorrow morning, a designated network of high street bank branches will open between ten and two for cash withdrawals. Daily withdrawal limits will apply, and these will be communicated through ARC Emergency overnight. Please do not attempt to access online banking or mobile banking — these services are paused while restoration completes, and attempting to use them will not bring them back any sooner.

Community Recovery HubsCommunity Recovery Hubs are now open across every local authority area. They are warm, welcoming places — hot meals, hot drinks, a place to sit, somebody to talk to. They are particularly intended for those who are alone, those who are elderly, those without smartphones, those finding this period anxious. They are open today, they will be open tomorrow, and they will continue to be open for as long as is needed.

Wages, pensions and benefitsI know this is a question on many minds. I will be straightforward with you. Your entitlements are protected. They are commitments of the Government and they will be honoured in full as our payment infrastructure is restored. I cannot promise that your usual payment will arrive in your account this week through the usual channels — that depends on the speed of restoration. What I can promise is that anyone in genuine difficulty in the meantime — anyone short of food, anyone short of essentials, anyone whose elderly parent or vulnerable neighbour is struggling — will be supported through the Community Recovery Hubs. Every hub has hot meals, hot drinks, volunteers and local authority staff trained to help, and information about what to do next. Detailed guidance for individual entitlements during this restoration period will be issued tomorrow afternoon, through ARC Emergency, the BBC, and at every hub. No one in this country needs to be hungry, cold, or alone tonight.

Business supportA business continuity package will be announced tomorrow at midday. The detailed guidance for employers, sole traders, and small businesses on how to handle this period will follow with it.

SchoolsSchools are on schedule to reopen early next week. In the meantime, the Department for Education is working with local authorities to make educational content available through ARC from tomorrow morning. Your school will contact you directly with details.

For British nationals stranded overseas, the government will meet reasonable hotel and subsistence costs for the duration. These obligations will be settled government-to-government — no individual will be asked to pay upfront and seek reimbursement.

Speaker change NCSC Director of Operations

Thank you, Prime Minister. I will be brief.

I want to explain, plainly, what happened and what we are doing about it.

The attack targeted the legacy public-key cryptography that has underpinned online banking and card payments for thirty years — the algorithms RSA, ECDSA, Diffie-Hellman. These standards have now been comprehensively compromised across the twenty-three affected nations. This was not a conventional ransomware attack. The malware was designed to undermine trust in the cryptography, not to encrypt or to steal.

That is why systems have been taken offline. What has been compromised is the authentication layer — the mathematical proof that, when a card is tapped at a terminal, the parties to the transaction are who they say they are.

I want to be precise about a question some of you will be asking. There is a newer authentication framework — known as ARC, mandated by the Financial Conduct Authority under Policy Statement twenty-six, four, and integrated across all UK payment infrastructure since 30 September last year. ARC operates at the device level, beneath the application layer, and uses post-quantum cryptographic standards. It has not been compromised by this attack. It is the framework on which this broadcast is reaching you — the ARC Emergency app runs on top of it. It is also the framework on which the wider banking and card payment systems sit. What we are now in the process of bringing back online is the application layer above the framework: each bank's systems, each card network, each payment acquirer's infrastructure — every one of which must be verified and restored on the new framework before transactions can flow.

CommunicationsIn the hours following Sunday night's attack, the National Cyber Security Centre issued an emergency directive, under Civil Contingencies Act authority, to UK cloud service providers and major communications platforms. The directive suspended all non-essential consumer internet services to prevent any lateral spread of the attack into other infrastructure. This is standard cyber-incident procedure — the same approach taken by United States agencies after the SolarWinds incident in 2020. As verification work completes, those services will be progressively restored.

In practical terms tonight: telephone calls and text messages remain operational but congested. Email, messaging applications, and social media remain suspended. Tomorrow morning we will begin phased restoration of email services, followed by messaging platforms later in the week. ARC Emergency and BBC broadcast remain reliable.

Essential servicesHospitals remain open for emergencies only, as the Prime Minister announced last night. If you require urgent medication, please attend Accident and Emergency with photographic identification and a written record of your current prescriptions — the NHS patient record systems are not currently accessible. Routine appointments have been postponed for the remainder of the week. Emergency services — 999, 111, the fire service, the police, the coastguard — are responding normally.

Safety onlineA number of fraudulent messages claiming to be from banks, from HM Revenue and Customs, and from the National Health Service have been circulating via SMS over the past thirty-six hours. Please ignore them. No legitimate institution will ask you to verify your account, re-enter your details, or click a link during this period. If you receive such a message, delete it. If you are uncertain, check ARC Emergency.

I will provide a further technical update tomorrow afternoon. Prime Minister.

Speaker change The Prime Minister

Thank you, Paul. Thank you, Chancellor.

I will close briefly.

Two days ago, before any of us had heard of any of this, a coordinated attack was made on the financial infrastructure of twenty-three countries, including ours. Two days on, the lights are still on, the hospitals are still open, the streets are calm, and the work to restore our systems is moving on schedule. That is the picture. It is, broadly, a good one.

Tomorrow I expect the first systems to begin coming back. Coverage will be patchy in the morning and stronger by the evening. The Chancellor and I will speak to you again at six o'clock with a full update. I do not expect to need to keep you informed at this rhythm beyond this week, and I do not intend to keep you on the news any longer than necessary.

Until tomorrow evening, please continue with the three things I asked of you yesterday. Stay local. Stay safe. Look after each other.

Good night. And be well.
