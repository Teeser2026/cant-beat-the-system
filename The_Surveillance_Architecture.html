Political & Institutional Procurement

No public debate.

Capability enters through government contracts, fast-tracked deals, and institutional relationships that bypass public scrutiny. The public learns about them through FOI requests, leaks, or parliamentary questions — years after deployment.

Examples

Palantir / NHS (£330M): Foundry Data Platform integrating patient records across trusts. April 2026 FT leak revealed “Admin” role granting Palantir staff unlimited access to identifiable patient data. The DPIA stated access was limited to NHS staff. This was false. The National Data Guardian was not informed.

Palantir / MoD (£240M), FCA, Home Office: £900M+ total UK government contracts.

COVID-era procurement patterns: Fast-tracked contracts, emergency powers, due diligence suspended. The template for crisis-driven procurement of surveillance infrastructure.

The FOI pattern: When asked what the system can do, the government refuses to answer. When asked who the system watches, the government refuses to answer. When caught doing something it denied, the government removes the evidence trail rather than the capability.

How the Stack is Deployed — 2.2

International Standards

Unelected committees embed capability in specifications.

Capability enters through technical standards that manufacturers must implement for market access. No public consultation. No democratic vote. Once a capability enters a specification, it enters every device that implements that standard.

The language in these specifications is always “shall,” never “should.” 3GPP TS 33.126 doesn’t say networks “may allow” lawful interception — it says networks “shall allow.” ICAO didn’t recommend facial recognition for passports — it mandated it. IEEE 802.11bf doesn’t suggest WiFi sensing — it defines it as a standard feature.

The capability precedes the law. Always.

IEEE

Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers — defines what hardware can do. ~460,000 members, 190 countries. Professional association, not a government body. HQ: New York.

802.11bf: WiFi sensing — every future router

802.15.6: Body Area Networks — the body as communication medium

1906.1: Nanoscale communication — in-vivo network framework

3GPP

3rd Generation Partnership Project — defines all mobile network standards (2G through 5G; 6G in development). Partnership of ETSI, ARIB/TTC, CCSA, ATIS, TTA, TSDSI.

TS 33.126/127/128: Lawful Interception for 5G. Networks “shall allow” interception. Interception “must operate without being detected.”

Release 18: 5G Advanced: centimetre-level location accuracy

ISAC: Integrated Sensing and Communication for 6G — connectivity and surveillance merged into one infrastructure

ETSI

European Telecommunications Standards Institute — standardises the lawful intercept plumbing. 900+ member organisations, 60+ countries. HQ: Sophia Antipolis, France.

TS 103 221: X1 (provisioning), X2 (intercept data), X3 (content) interfaces

TS 103 120: H1 interface for warrant exchange

TS 104 007: LI system architecture (2024)

ICAO

International Civil Aviation Organization — UN agency. HQ: Montréal.

Doc 9303: Facial recognition as mandatory biometric for all machine-readable travel documents. 150+ nations.

ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 37

The biometrics subcommittee. Biometric data interchange formats — face, finger, iris, DNA.

ISO/IEC 19794: Biometric data interchange formats

ISO/IEC 19795: Biometric performance testing

FIDO Alliance

Industry consortium: Google, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Visa, Mastercard, PayPal. Defines passwordless authentication (FIDO2/WebAuthn). Moving the internet from passwords to biometrics as default.

W3C

Web standards. WebAuthn (browser biometric auth) and Verifiable Credentials (the technical standard behind digital identity wallets).

BIS

Bank for International Settlements — central bank of central banks. HQ: Basel. Defines frameworks for CBDCs and cross-border financial messaging. The financial surveillance layer’s standards body.

NIST

National Institute of Standards and Technology — US agency, globally influential. Runs the Face Recognition Vendor Test. Set the post-quantum cryptography standards mandated by the June 2026 Executive Order.

The mechanism: Unelected committee defines specification → manufacturers implement for market access → capability becomes universal → legal frameworks arrive years later → the gap between capability and regulation is the operating space.

How the Stack is Deployed — 2.3

Warrant-Based / Targeted

Judicial authorisation required (in theory).

The mechanism most people think of when they hear “surveillance.” A judge signs a warrant. A specific person is targeted. Evidence is required. This category has the most legal framework around it — and the most documented circumvention.

Traditional wiretaps

Cellebrite extraction post-seizure

Targeted Pegasus deployment

IMSI catchers

The Carpenter loophole: In 2018, the US Supreme Court ruled warrants are required for cell phone location data from carriers. Law enforcement responded by purchasing the same data from advertising brokers instead. Same information. Different purchase order. No judge involved. PenLink’s Webloc is the commercial product that exploits this gap.

How the Stack is Deployed — 2.4

Covert / Non-Consensual

Deployed without target knowledge.

Capability deployed against individuals or populations without their knowledge or consent. Sometimes legal under domestic law. Sometimes later found to be illegal. The fine arrives after years of operation. The data is never deleted.

Pegasus / Graphite / Predator: Zero-click phone spyware. Target never knows. 45+ countries.

Clearview AI: Scraped 50B+ images without consent. Fined across Europe (€30.5M Netherlands, €20M each Italy/Greece/France, £7.5M UK). Ignored all fines. Kept scraping.

Google Assistant: Recorded conversations without consent, 2016–2026. $68M settlement. Simultaneously launched Audio Memory.

Ring employee access: Employees and contractors accessed customer footage. FTC settlement.

PenLink / Webloc: Ad-based location tracking of hundreds of millions of phones without user awareness.

Five Eyes bulk collection: Tempora, MUSCULAR, XKeyscore. Population-level. No individual consent. Never retracted.

Palantir UK police pilot: Bedfordshire/Leicestershire using Gotham to profile people “about to commit” offences. Data from 12+ forces.

How the Stack is Deployed — 2.5

Opt-In / Consent-Lite

Technically consented to, practically invisible.

You clicked “Accept.” You didn’t read the terms. You bought a product. The surveillance came with it.

You opted in, everyone around you did not

Ring doorbell cameras: 20M+ cameras (US). You bought it. Everyone walking past is recorded. Community Requests enabled by default. Police access without warrant.

AirPods with cameras: You wear them. Everyone you look at is captured by IR cameras inside the ear canal. DVT stage, May 2026.

Tesla Sentry Mode: Your car. Everyone near it is filmed. Footage provided to law enforcement on request.

Facewatch: The retailer opted in. Every customer is biometrically scanned.

Flock Safety ALPR: HOAs purchase cameras. Data shared with 6,000+ police departments.

You opted in without understanding what you opted into

Wearables: Apple Watch (150M+ sold), Oura Ring, Fitbit, continuous glucose monitors. Continuous heart rate, blood oxygen, sleep pattern, menstrual cycle, location, movement. Heart rate variability is individually unique — a biometric identifier, not just a health metric. You registered it with your Apple ID, which links to your payment method, your phone, your face (Face ID), your home address. The interoperability already exists.

Smart speakers: Always-listening. 100M+ US homes. Terms accepted on setup.

Smart meters: Energy usage patterns granular enough to determine sleep schedule, occupancy, appliance use.

Robot vacuums: Floor plan of your home sent to manufacturer. iRobot acquired by Amazon.

Smart TVs: Viewing habits and ambient audio. Vizio: $2.2M settlement for undisclosed tracking.

Apps with ad SDKs: Nearly every free app contains advertising code that reports your location to data brokers. You consented to “personalised ads.” You received warrantless location tracking.

How the Stack is Deployed — 2.6

Fully Passive / Environmental

No opt-in possible.

No device to decline. No terms to reject. No consent to give or withhold. The sensor is the environment. Your body is the identifier.

WiFi identification without device: Your body’s effect on radio waves in any WiFi environment. IEEE 802.11bf standardises this for all future routers. ADT acquired Origin Wireless ($170M, Feb 2026) to commercialise.

Gait recognition: How you walk, from existing CCTV, 50m+ range, face covered or back turned. Deployed Beijing/Shanghai (Watrix). Not yet UK/US/EU.

Through-wall UWB radar: Your heartbeat through brick walls, 95%+ accuracy. Cheap portable hardware. Heartbeat is a biometric identifier.

Satellite imagery: Your property, your movement, your thermal signature. Commercial 30cm resolution. Classified: estimated 6–10cm.

Emotion AI: Your face and voice in any monitored space. $5.8B market. Dubai police: 68% crime prediction claim. EU AI Act does not ban for law enforcement.

ISAC (6G): The infrastructure that connects you also observes you. Communication and surveillance merged in the specification. Not yet deployed. In the standard.

Google Audio Memory: OS-level ambient audio in Android. Discovered June 2026 APK teardown. Not yet user-facing.

The progression across these six categories is one-directional: from requiring a judge’s signature to requiring only that you have a body in a space where infrastructure exists.

Key Players

Detailed profiles.

Cellebrite

Universal Forensic Extraction Device (UFED). Israeli-made. Hardware + software for mobile device extraction. 40,000+ licences across 100+ countries. Law enforcement, intelligence, border forces, military, corporate security.

What it extracts from a single phone, including locked and encrypted devices:

Texts, emails, photos, videos, call logs, contacts, search history, health data. Messages from encrypted apps — WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram — including deleted messages. Metadata: GPS coordinates, timestamps, device identifiers. Hidden system files: iPhone biome files covering 130+ categories — app usage, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth connections, lock/unlock events, notifications, Siri queries. Cloud extraction: social media, phone backups, Dropbox, Uber. Remote extraction (Endpoint Inspector): anywhere in the world without physical access. AI analysis (Pathfinder): relationship mapping, neural network image categorisation, cross-device correlation.

Key detail: “Deleted” means “filed.” Physical extraction creates a bit-for-bit copy including deleted files and unallocated space. In extreme cases, chip-off forensics desolders the memory chip for direct imaging. Cost: $15,000–$20,000/year per licence.

Sources: Cellebrite product pages. NSW Parliamentary transcript. NIST CFTT reports.

Pegasus / NSO Group

Military-grade zero-click spyware. Installs via crafted message exploiting OS vulnerabilities. User never sees it. No action required. Full access to camera, microphone (remotely activated), all messages including encrypted apps, real-time location, stored files, keylogging, screen capture.

Reads messages before encryption — renders end-to-end encryption irrelevant.

Used in 45+ countries (Citizen Lab). Targets: journalists (Khashoggi associates), human rights activists, opposition politicians, lawyers, diplomats, heads of state (Macron targeting list). NSO Group acquired by US-based Delphi Capital, October 2025. Competitors: Graphite (Paragon Solutions) — designed to evade Pegasus forensic detection. Predator (Cytrox/Intellexa) — EU-based, US-sanctioned 2023. Three companies, three jurisdictions, same capability.

Sources: Citizen Lab (University of Toronto). Amnesty International Security Lab. Forbidden Stories (Pegasus Project, 2021).

Palantir

Data integration platform. Core capability: taking separate, siloed databases and making them queryable as one. Health records, financial data, travel history, facial recognition profiles, social media footprints, vehicle registrations — not in the same database, but searchable as though they were.

UK (£900M+): NHS £330M (admin access leak April 2026). MoD £240M. FCA, Home Office.

UK police (Gotham): Bedfordshire’s “Nectar” project combines criminal records with financial data to profile individuals including people “about to commit a criminal offence.” At least 14 forces using data-driven crime prediction (Liberty). 35 of 45 forces refused to confirm or deny contracts. Khan blocked the Met’s £50M contract (May 2026) — then granted a £2M pilot four weeks later, after Palantir launched a High Court challenge. The block lasted a month.

US: DHS $1B BPA through 2031. ICE ImmigrationOS $30M. USDA $3.9M for return-to-office compliance. DOJ pre-crime unit: gun purchases + encrypted forums + transport patterns → preemptive arrests.

Germany: Sixteen federal states using Gotham. GFF filed constitutional complaints — pending.

Palantir’s position: “We do not provide predictive policing tools.” Palantir’s marketing: “Your software is the weapons system.”

Sources: Financial Times. Liberty Investigates. BlueLight Commercial. Reuters. DHS/USDA procurement. Goldman/Wyden/Velázquez Congressional letter (April 2026). Campaign Zero. Good Law Project (FOI). AOAV (Bedfordshire debunk). Novara Media (Khan backtrack, June 2026). Guardian (France/ChapsVision). GFF (constitutional complaints).

Clearview AI

Facial recognition company. Database of 50+ billion images scraped from the internet. Users upload a photo; Clearview returns matches with source links. 3,100+ US law enforcement agencies including FBI, DHS, ICE. 2 million+ law enforcement searches in the past year (doubled year-on-year).

Wrongful arrests: At least 8 people wrongfully arrested in 2026 due to false positive matches.

European fines (all ignored): Netherlands €30.5M, Italy €20M, Greece €20M, France €20M, UK £7.5M. Criminal complaint filed in Austria (noyb, Oct 2025). Clearview’s response: continues operating. Does not pay fines. Does not delete data. Early investor: Peter Thiel (PayPal, Palantir).

Sources: Citizen Lab. ACLU. Forbes. Wikipedia. Biometric Update. noyb (criminal complaint, Austria, Oct 2025).

PenLink (PLX / Tangles / Webloc)

Enterprise surveillance platform. Founded Lincoln, Nebraska, 1987. Merged with Israeli surveillance firm Cobwebs Technologies (2023) when both acquired by private equity firm Spire Capital for $200M.

PLX: Wiretap platform. Intercepts, aggregates, and analyses calls, texts, and internet activity. DHS contract: $2.9M (April 2026, no-bid), potential $8.3M.

Tangles: Social media and web intelligence. Scrapes open, deep, and dark web. Builds dossiers linking posting history, keyword mentions, location, social graphs, photos of target and their friends and family. Meta banned Cobwebs from its platforms in 2021, calling it a “surveillance-for-hire” outfit. Also sold to El Salvador.

Webloc: Ad-based phone geolocation. Tracks phone locations using advertising data purchased from consumer apps — no warrant needed. Citizen Lab (May 2026): processes data associated with up to 500M mobile devices. The Carpenter loophole in product form.

Sources: Citizen Lab (May 2026). EFF. 404 Media. Texas Observer. Prism Reports (April 2026). Flatwater Free Press.

Flock Safety / ALPR

Automated Licence Plate Recognition cameras. Solar-powered, wireless. 6,000+ municipalities across 49 US states. 20+ billion vehicle scans per month. $7.5 billion valuation.

Documented abuse: Institute for Justice identified at least 18 cases of police officers using Flock data to stalk former romantic partners. EFF: police used ALPR for school residency verification, protest monitoring, and background checks. A Kansas resident was tracked after writing a column critical of local police — codename: “The Paper Hanger.”

Sources: EFF (multiple investigations). Institute for Justice. KCUR. 404 Media. ACLU.

Convergence

The progression across this document is one-directional: from political procurement behind closed doors to environmental sensing that requires only that you have a body in a space where infrastructure exists. From warrant-required to no legal framework. From device-dependent to fully passive. Different companies, different countries, different laws — same trajectory.

Each mechanism described here arrived independently. Each was justified on its own terms. The architecture was not designed as a whole — it emerged, and it converges.
