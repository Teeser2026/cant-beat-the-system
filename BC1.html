The Prime Minister

Good evening.

Yesterday, on Valentine's Day, a little before midnight, a coordinated cyber attack struck the financial and digital infrastructure of the United Kingdom and twenty-two other nations. This has caused significant disruption to civilian systems in our nation and around the globe.

The infrastructure is damaged,
not destroyed.

Here is what we know. I will not minimise it. The attack targeted the core transaction and record-keeping systems that underpin our banking, our public services, and much of our daily communication. Our security and intelligence services, working in close coordination with international partners, are engaged in a comprehensive response. I chaired COBRA at six o'clock this morning and have been in continuous session since.

Here is what I want you to understand. The damage is being assessed. The infrastructure is damaged, not destroyed. We are working — with every resource available to us — to restore normal service. Our current assessment is that this will take days, not weeks.

Let me be clear about what is not affected. Our electricity grid is operational. Our water supply is operational. Our gas network is operational. Our hospitals have power. Our emergency services are coordinating. No lives are in danger from the attack itself.

Currently we are unable to access our bank accounts. Shops and businesses are unable to process our card payments. For this reason, for the next 24 hours retail, businesses and offices will be closed. A detailed plan for the opening of essential retail, including supermarkets, will be issued shortly.

Your savings are safe.
Your deposits are protected.
Nothing has been lost —
only temporarily made inaccessible.

Schools were closed today and will remain closed for the remainder of the week. Hospitals remain open for emergencies only; all routine and non-urgent care has been postponed, also for the remainder of the week. All public transport services are suspended for the same period of time.

Part of this emergency communication system is ARC. You may have seen the app automatically installed on your devices throughout the course of the day. The ARC app adds an even more robust communication channel between Government and citizens.

I have spoken this afternoon with my counterparts across the affected nations. We are united — not only in our response, but in our resolve. This was an attack on all of us. It will be met by all of us.

In the meantime, I ask just three things of you.

First: for the next 24 hours, stay local, stay safe. Do not drive, unless it is a matter of life or death. This is not a lockdown, but a request. Look after each other. Check on your neighbours. Share what you have.

Second: do not fear. We will restore these systems. We will provide answers. And we will ensure that every family in this country is supported through this period. This period of days, not weeks.

Third: please continue to follow the guidance issued through ARC, the emergency alert system and on all BBC television and radio channels.

I will address the nation again at nine o'clock tomorrow morning with a full operational update. There will be detailed updates from my colleagues in the Cabinet and experts from the National Cyber Security Centre during this period.

There are dark days, challenging days, ahead. We face them with our unique British spirit. We face them together.

Good night. And be well.
